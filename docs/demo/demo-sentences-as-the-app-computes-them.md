# The ten demo sentences, as the app computes them

For the run of show and the deck: the number each demo sentence produces, read off the shipped
database and the rules engine, not from anyone's memory. Priya, 20 September 2026, evening.

## How these were produced, and what they are not

`DemoSentencesTest` (`app/src/test/.../orchestration/`) drives all twenty rows of Jacob's
`data-authoring/demo-utterance-set.csv` (ten sentences, Hindi and English columns) through the
three deterministic stages of a turn, on the JVM:

1. `IntentRouter.decide` on the reference transcript: which intent, and whether the words
   decided it or the model would have been asked.
2. `LookupMealResolver` over `SqliteFoodLookup` on the bundled `katori-food.db`: which food each
   spoken name resolves to, the grams the household unit becomes, and the figures.
3. `DefaultRulesEngine.evaluate` with the demo profile (22 years, 62 kg, 168 cm, hostel student,
   no diet type, nothing declared except in #9), the candidate list of spec 4.3
   (`data-authoring/candidates.csv`, `0030`: what a hostel student can actually get, scored per
   serving), and for beat 4 a placeholder lab value with the shape of the report (fasting
   glucose 118 mg/dL, printed range 70 to 100).

**Not in it:** speech (the reference column IS the transcript; the recogniser's real output is
Jacob's measurement), and the model. The extraction is authored in the test as what the
extraction prompt is asked to produce from each sentence ("two rotis" is `roti × 2`, "a katori
of dal" is `dal × 1 katori`), and it is labelled so in the source. What the model actually
extracts on the phone is Rao's end-to-end run. The figures below are therefore the figures the
plate WILL show if extraction is right, and the figures the model may quote in beats 2 to 4.

The test asserts: every sentence is decided by the words (no model call to route), every food
resolves to the food the demo means, a counted egg weighs one egg, and the knowledge rows for
beats 2 and 3 are found in both languages. It fails on any regression. Regenerate with the
standalone runner (`tools/jvm-tests-standalone.sh`); the table is also written to
`logs/demo-sentences-as-the-app-computes-them.md` on every run.

## Read this first: what the run found and what changed

- **Every Hindi row went to the model on the first run.** The router's markers were Latin
  script only; the Devanagari the hi recogniser emits (क्या, कितना, चाहिए, खाया, कटोरी …) meant
  nothing to it. Devanagari markers, log words and quantity words are in now, every one
  GENERATED by someone who does not read Hindi and listed on `log-words-review.md` for Vedant to
  verify. Since Meera's 18:07 finding the demo stands on the `en-IN` rows; the Hindi rows are
  insurance.
- **Three sentences have no eating verb** (#2 hi, #4, #5: "Two rotis, a katori of dal, and two
  spoons of oil"; "Two rotis no, three rotis and dal"; "One plate of rice, dal and a bowl of
  curd"). They were "no evidence, ask the model", which is a 5 s classifier call and a coin toss
  on beat 1. A quantity or a household unit beside a food with no question marker now counts as
  a meal stated, so all three are decided LOG by the words. Cost: "three days no rice" would log
  rice. Accepted: a sentence like that is not a demo sentence, and a LOG on a question is still
  refused.
- **"One boiled egg" weighed 150 g.** The egg was classed DAIRY "so the katori and piece rules
  apply"; DAIRY has no piece rule, so an egg with no unit was a katori. It is now classed as
  chicken is, and one egg is one 50 g piece (the shipped default, shown as such). #3's protein
  went from 25.1 g to 12.6 g. This was the one wrong number in the set.
- **Idli and sambar had no Devanagari alias**: #10 hi was NO_MATCH. Added.
- **The Hindi beat 2 found no knowledge row** ("प्रोटीन" is not "protein" to the retriever).
  Devanagari nutrient words are read as their English tag now; #7 hi finds the same two rows as
  #7 en.
- **Same sentence, different bowls (#5):** the English column says "a bowl of curd" (200 g), the
  Hindi says "एक कटोरी दही" (150 g), so the two columns do not agree on the figures. Neither is
  wrong. If the deck quotes #5, quote one column; or Jacob changes the English to "a katori of
  curd", which is what the presenter would say anyway.
- **Two spoons of oil is 20 g** (a spoon is 10 g in the unit table), not the 10 g in the scripted
  feed. The scripted feed's figures are someone's memory; the ones below are the database's.
- **The curd was American low-fat yogurt with added milk solids** (found by the US-record sweep
  that the bread started, `data-authoring/us-record-sweep.csv`, 93 records, 5 acted on): protein
  5.25 and fat 1.55 per 100 g where home-set curd is 3.47 and 3.25. #5's katori of curd lost
  2.7 g of protein it never had; the row below is the whole-milk record. The milk record is now
  the unfortified twin (same figures). White bread ships no iron figure (its US iron is
  enrichment) and says so beside its figures; buttermilk is the chaas recipe (curd thinned with
  water), a third of the US cultured record's energy. If a judge asks why the app thinks Indian
  bread has American iron in it: it does not, and the sweep file is the list of what was checked.
- **Beat 3 and beat 4 rankings.** Ranked by iron per 100 g over the whole database the top
  candidates for anaemia were cumin, turmeric and bay leaf; per serving (`02417a9`), raw cowpea,
  raw urad and raw masoor. Ruled: raw grains and pulses are ingredients, not candidates. Spec
  4.3's list is authored (`0030`): what a hostel student can get from a canteen and a shop, 58
  of 157 keys. **Beat 3 with the list: cooked chana dal, palakura pappu, thotakura pappu, chana
  masala, dal tadka.** That is the row a slide can carry. (A slice of bread, not a 150 g katori
  of it, or US iron-enriched white bread tops the list.)
  **Beat 4 is still the engine's**: with "fibre higher, carbohydrate lower" every zero-carbohydrate
  item scores zero and ties (water, boiled egg, fried egg, alphabetically), and every real food
  scores below zero because the carbohydrate term is ten times the fibre term per serving.
  `RankingDefectsTest` (domain/) is RED ON MASTER BY DESIGN for Rao, three tests, with a fourth
  that guards the fix: a ranked candidate has a positive score, and the two terms must be
  comparable (a per-nutrient rank), or the fix empties beat 4.
  With a percentile per nutrient the hostel list gives fresh coconut, cooked moong dal, carrot,
  beetroot, chicken curry. **Until Rao's fix lands, the run of show should not read beat 4's
  candidates aloud; the trigger sentence is right and is the line to read.**
