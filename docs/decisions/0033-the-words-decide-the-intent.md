# 0033. The words decide the intent; the model is consulted only when they cannot

Date: 20 September 2026. Status: accepted, ruled by Vedant. Supersedes the "one short prompt"
design of `0020` as the primary router; the prompt stays as the fallback.

## The evidence

The integrator ran the classifier prompt on the authored 53-case set on the phone
(`logs/hw-report-conversational.txt`, `b4_intentSet`): **23 of 53 right**, identical at 4 and 8
threads, 4.5 to 5.3 s per case at 192 prompt tokens. Wrong more often than right. The misroutes
mostly went to RECOMMEND, which is the safe direction; **three ANSWERs went to LOG**, which
writes a meal the person never ate into their diary. In the demo, beat 2 corrupts beat 1 in
front of the judges. `RoutingDefectsTest` reproduces it on the JVM: "how many rotis have I eaten
today" with a model LOG verdict puts two rotis into the store.

## The ruling

Four intents in a food-logging app is a decision, not a reading, and the design claim of this
product is that the model does not decide. So the router is deterministic-first:

1. **Markers decide when they are unambiguous**, which is most real utterances. `IntentRouter`
   reads the sentence for evidence of each intent: a certain log (`LogPrefilter`), a question
   about what they ate (a question marker with a past-tense eating word, or "did I", "yesterday",
   "my lunch", "kal"), a plate in front of them ("having", "about to", "should I add", "kha
   raha", "abhi"), or asking what they should eat ("what should I eat", "for more iron", "to
   lose weight", "kya khana chahiye", "ke liye"). Exactly one kind of evidence decides the
   intent with no model call. A general question with none of them is ANSWER (`0020`).
2. **The model is consulted only when the words are silent or conflict**, and then only for the
   intents the words allowed.
3. **A LOG verdict on any sentence with a question marker is refused**, whatever the model
   says, and the person is asked. This is the line that makes the demo-breaking misroute
   impossible, and it ships first and on its own if nothing else does.

This also takes a 4.5 to 5.3 s model call off the hot path for every sentence the words decide,
which is most of them.

## Measured, on the JVM

`IntentRouterTest`, on the authored 53-case set:

    decided by the words       51 of 53   (96.2 %)   <- no model call
    decided WRONGLY             0                    <- asserted at zero
    question decided as LOG     0                    <- asserted at zero: this writes a meal
    sent to the model           2                    "a plate of vegetable biryani and some raita",
                                                     "tea with two biscuits": no verb, no marker

On a batch of 17 sentences written afterwards to break it (the nearest thing to a held-out set
the same hand can write): 14 decided right, 3 sent to the model with the right intent allowed,
0 wrong. Three weaknesses the batch found are fixed in the lists: "today" and "aaj" are as often
about the next meal as the last and are no longer history evidence; Hindi "pi sakta hoon" (can
I drink) is not "drank"; "I want to eat something for iron" had no recommend evidence.

**Both sets are circular** and the numbers are regression guards, not accuracy. The recorded
transcripts replace them, five or six speakers, a small sample.

## Languages

The demo runs in Hindi or Indian English, ruled today. The English and roman Hindi markers
(kya, kitna, kaise, kab, chahiye, ke liye, kha raha, abhi, aaj, kal, maine) are the ones that
carry weight, and the ones Vedant can check himself without a reviewer. The roman Telugu
entries were generated by someone who does not read Telugu, are marked as such in the source,
and are not load-bearing for the 26th. The asymmetry holds throughout: a wrong marker costs a
model call or an "ask", never a written meal, because LOG is decided only by the pre-filter and
refused on any question.

## What the integrator wires

One line, where the orchestrator consults the model today:

    decided ?: (IntentRouter.decide(text) as? Decided)?.intent?.toDomain()
      ?: run { classify(text) ...; IntentRouter.accept(c.value, text)?.toDomain() ?: NeedsIntent(text) }

`RoutingDefectsTest` is red on master until it lands: a question the model calls LOG must be
refused and asked; a sentence the words can route must not consult the model; a sentence with no
evidence still must. `DefaultOrchestratorTest`'s "a question never skips the classifier" asserts
the old behaviour and changes with the wiring.

## What this does not claim

That the words decide correctly on speech nobody has recorded yet. That the two sentences
sent to the model come back right. That 96.2% is accuracy. The next number is the recorded
speakers through `IntentRouter.decide`, misroutes first.
