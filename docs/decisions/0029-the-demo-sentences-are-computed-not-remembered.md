# 0029. The demo sentences are computed, not remembered; a quantity beside a food is a meal stated

Date: 20 September 2026. Status: accepted. Ruled by Vedant: "run the ten demo sentences end to
end, minus speech, and report per sentence"; "if any demo sentence routes wrongly or resolves
badly, say so loudly; we change the sentence, not the system, at this distance from the day".

## What was done

`DemoSentencesTest` pushes all twenty rows of `data-authoring/demo-utterance-set.csv` (ten
sentences, Hindi and English) through `IntentRouter.decide`, `LookupMealResolver` on the shipped
database, and `DefaultRulesEngine` with the demo profile. Extraction is authored as the prompt
asks for it and labelled so; speech and the model are not in the loop. The table it writes is
`docs/demo/demo-sentences-as-the-app-computes-them.md`, for the run of show and the deck. It
replaces the scripted feed's figures, which were someone's memory (roti 80 g, oil 10 g), with
the database's (a chapati serving is 45 g, a spoon of oil is 10 g, so two are 20 g).

## What the first run found, and the four decisions it forced

1. **Every Hindi row went to the model.** The markers of `0027` were Latin script: the hi
   recogniser writes Devanagari. Devanagari question, advice, log and quantity words are in the
   three lists now (`LogPrefilter`, `IntentRouter`, `SafetyLine`) and the knowledge retriever
   reads Devanagari nutrient words as their English tag. All GENERATED by a hand that does not
   read Hindi, listed on `log-words-review.md`; Vedant reads Devanagari and verifies. Since
   Meera's 18:07 finding (0019 addendum 7) the demo stands on the English rows; these are
   insurance, not load-bearing.

2. **A quantity or a household unit beside a food, with no question marker, is a meal stated.**
   Three demo sentences have no eating verb ("Two rotis, a katori of dal, and two spoons of
   oil"; "Two rotis no, three rotis and dal"; "One plate of rice, dal and a bowl of curd"), and
   the pre-filter of `0020` required one, so they were "no evidence, ask the model": a 5 s
   classifier call and a coin toss on beat 1. `LogPrefilter.isCertainLog` now accepts a number,
   a number word (one to ten, ek to das, एक to दस) or a household unit (katori, plate, glass,
   spoon, ml, gram, and their Hindi forms) as positive evidence. The asymmetry of `0027` holds:
   a question marker still wins, so "how much protein in two rotis" is a question and "I need
   two rotis" is a request; and a LOG verdict on any question is still refused. The cost, named:
   "three days no rice" would log rice. **Handled, ruled with the rule (Vedant, evening):** a
   negation near the food blocks the LOG regardless, from the words and from the model. "no
   rice", "didn't eat", "nahi khaya", "skipped", "nothing", and their Devanagari and Telugu
   forms (`LogPrefilter.NEGATIONS`, on the reviewer's sheet). Logging a meal somebody just said
   they did not eat is the worst version of this failure, because the person is actively telling
   us the opposite. The one exception is a correction of the count, which the demo's #4 is:
   "two rotis no, three rotis and dal" has the negation followed directly by a quantity, and is
   a meal.

3. **A counted egg is one egg.** "One boiled egg" weighed 150 g: the egg was classed DAIRY "so
   the katori and piece rules apply", and DAIRY has no piece rule, so an egg with no unit took
   the class's usual unit, a katori. Reclassed as chicken already is, `COMPOSED_DISH`, whose
   usual unit is a 50 g piece. #3's protein went from 25.1 g to 12.6 g.

   **This is the find of the night, and the whole argument for the exercise, in Vedant's
   words: 150 g for one boiled egg, protein 25.1 where the truth is 12.6, on a sentence we were
   going to say out loud on stage. Nothing in the test suite would have caught it, because
   every test agreed with the classification. Only running the exact sentences found it.**
   The matcher tests proved "boiled egg" resolves to `egg`; the resolver tests proved a DAIRY
   item with no unit takes a katori; the importer proved the egg row was well formed. Every
   part was right by its own test and the plate was wrong. The demo sentences are therefore a
   fixed test in the suite from now on, asserting the figures a judge will read, not the parts.

   The same run also found, with the same shape, the quantity rule (2) and the negation rule
   below: the words tests passed on the authored set and three demo sentences still went to
   the model.

4. **Same sentence, two bowls.** #5's English column says "a bowl of curd" (200 g) and its Hindi
   column "एक कटोरी दही" (150 g); the figures differ by column and neither is wrong. Per the
   ruling, the sentence changes, not the unit table: the recommendation to Jacob is "a katori of
   curd", and until then the deck quotes one column.

## What is not fixed here, and whose it is

Ranked by nutrient per 100 g over the whole database, the beat 3 candidates for anaemia were
cumin, turmeric and bay leaf; Vedant ruled candidates rank per serving (`02417a9`), which puts
raw cowpea, raw urad and raw masoor at the top: a raw pulse's serving is a 200 g cup. Raw grains
and pulses are ingredients, not candidates; that is spec 4.3's per-context list, unauthored and
Priya's. Per serving does not fix beat 4 either: with "fibre higher, carbohydrate lower" every item with no carbohydrate and
no fibre scores exactly zero and ties, and the alphabetical tiebreak presents raw chicken breast,
brewed coffee, raw carp and water under a correct trigger sentence. `RankingDefectsTest` is red
on master by design for the integrator: a ranked candidate has a positive score, by the engine's
own "no preference, no suggestions" reasoning.
