# 0021. IndicConformer loads and is not garbage; the English model is not IndicConformer

Date: 20 September 2026. Status: accepted for the code and the desktop findings. NOT a device
claim: nothing in this record ran on a phone.

## The question this answers

`0005` chose `parismitaglobalsolutions/indicconformer-sherpa-onnx` for all three ASR languages,
recorded that it is one person's export with four likes and no downloads, that its Apache-2.0
claim is its own, and that it had never been loaded. `HANDOVER.md` §7 lists it as a risk the whole
ASR plan rests on. The brief for this slice said: if it will not load or produces garbage, stop and
report, because that is a product finding rather than a bug.

It loads, and it is not garbage. The evidence is below, and so is what else the files turned out
to be.

## What the files are, read from their own ONNX metadata

Opened with ONNX Runtime 1.23.1 on the desktop, x64. Every value is `custom_metadata_map` from the
file itself, not the repository README.

| file | `model_author` | `model_type` | `vocab_size` | `subsampling_factor` | comment |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `te/model.int8.onnx` | ai4bharat | EncDecCTCModelBPE | 5633 | 4 | "ai4bharat indicconformer, te, CTC-only, masked, int8 MatMul-only" |
| `hi/model.int8.onnx` | ai4bharat | EncDecCTCModelBPE | 5633 | 4 | "ai4bharat/indicconformer_stt_hi_hybrid_ctc_rnnt_large, CTC-only, Hindi-masked, int8" |
| `en/model.int8.onnx` | **NeMo** | EncDecHybridRNNTCTCBPEModel | **1024** | **8** | "Only the CTC branch is exported"; `url` = NGC `stt_en_fastconformer_hybrid_large_pc` |

All three carry the metadata keys sherpa-onnx's NeMo CTC path reads (`vocab_size`,
`normalize_type=per_feature`, `subsampling_factor`), input `audio_signal [batch, 80, time]` plus
`length`, output `logprobs`. The te and hi output dimension is 5633, which is exactly the line count
of the repository-root `tokens.txt` (`<blk>` at index 5632), and a script census of that file
shows Devanagari, Bengali, Arabic, Meetei, Tamil, Ol Chiki, Malayalam, Kannada, Oriya, Telugu,
Gurmukhi and Gujarati. So `0005`'s note that te and hi share the root `tokens.txt` is confirmed.

**FINDING, contradicting `0005`: the English model is NVIDIA's stock English FastConformer, not an
AI4Bharat model.** Its provenance, and therefore its licence, is NVIDIA's NGC card for
`stt_en_fastconformer_hybrid_large_pc`, not the repository's Apache-2.0 claim. NGC NeMo models are
generally CC-BY-4.0; that is to be read off the card, not assumed here, and `0005`'s table needs
the row corrected either way. Its handle id in `AsrModels` is `asr.fastconformer-en` so the code
does not repeat the mistake. Consequence for the product: it has a 1,024-piece English vocabulary
and cannot say "pappu"; see the English rows below.

## The smoke test

sherpa-onnx 1.13.8, Python, `OfflineRecognizer.from_nemo_ctc`, greedy search, 4 threads, on the
files `logs/model-fetch.log` checksummed. Laptop: i5-13420H, Windows 11, shared with five other
sessions' Gradle daemons. Script: `tools/asr_eval.py`; clips regenerated by its `synth` command;
report in `logs/asr-eval-synthetic.log`.

**Every clip is SYNTHETIC and the set is circular by construction** (`0006`, `MatchRateTest`). The
Telugu clips are the Piper `te_IN-padmavathi` voice the app ships for TTS, which was trained on
IndicVoices-R, the same AI4Bharat data family the recogniser was; that is the optimistic case. The
English clips are Windows SAPI voices with no Indian accent. These numbers show the model works.
They are not accuracy, and they replace nothing in spec 10.6. The recorded meal logs do.

### Telugu model, Telugu speech

| clip | reference | hypothesis | errors |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| rotis_pappu | నేను రెండు రొట్టెలు మరియు పప్పు తిన్నాను | same | exact |
| idli_sambar | ఉదయం మూడు ఇడ్లీ సాంబార్ తిన్నాను | ఉదయం మూడు ఇడిలీ సాంబార్ తిన్నాను | 1 word, 1 char |
| codemix | టూ రోటీస్ అండ్ పప్పు తిన్నాను | టూ రోటీస్ అండ పప్పు తిన్నాను | 1 word, 1 char |
| milk_egg | ఒక గ్లాసు పాలు మరియు ఒక ఉడికించిన గుడ్డు తిన్నాను | same | exact |

WER 8.3 %, CER 1.5 % over 4 clips. Both errors are a single vowel sign or virama; every food name
survived. An earlier run of the same texts (Piper is not bit-deterministic between runs) had
rotis_pappu, idli_sambar and milk_egg exact and codemix off by one character in the same word
(అండా for అండ్).

### English model, English speech

| clip | reference | hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| zira_rotis | I ate two rotis and dal for lunch | I ate two rotees in Dal for lunch. |
| david_idli | I had three idli and sambar for breakfast | I had three idly and sombre for breakfast. |
| hazel_mixed | two rotis and pappu with a glass of milk | two ruttis and pap you with a glass of milk. |
| zira_egg | I drank 200 ml of milk and ate one boiled egg | I drank two hundred ml of milk and ate one boiled egg |

WER 25.0 %, CER 17.3 %. Every error but "200"/"two hundred" (a normalisation choice the script
documents) is an Indic food name rendered as the nearest English words. This is the code-mixing
problem `0005` and spec 10.2 predicted, in the flesh, and it is a property of a 1,024-piece English
vocabulary, not of these voices. Downstream, "idly" is one edit from idli and "rotees" two from
rotis; "sombre" and "pap you" are not recoverable by the matcher. Whether the LLM recovers them is
what `AsrDeviceTest.c` measures.

### Telugu and Hindi models, English speech

Both transliterate English into their script: te gave `ఐ ట్రాంక్ టు హండ్రెడ్ ఎమల్ అవ్ మల్` for "I
drank two hundred ml of mil(k)"; hi gave `आई हैव टी आइडली एनसामबर फॉर ब्रेकफिस्ट` for "I have
three idli an(d) sambar for breakfast". Coherent phonetic output in the wrong script. It is what a
script-masked Indic model does and it says the weights are sane; it is not a measurement of
anything a user will do.

### Non-speech, and what it means for the engine

Telugu model, 3 s each, digital inputs:

| input | pieces | text |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| silence | 2 | ఈారు |
| white noise, quiet | 1 | అ |
| white noise, loud | 0 | (empty) |
| 50 Hz hum | 1 | అ |
| speech, clean, 2.46 s | 22 | exact |
| speech at 10 dB SNR | 22 | exact |
| speech at 0 dB SNR | 20 | 2 of 6 words wrong |
| speech at -20 dB gain | 22 | exact |
| speech clipped 8x | 22 | exact |
| speech, first 0.3 s only | 3 | నేను, correct |

**The model emits phantom pieces on silence.** An engine that treated a non-empty string as a
transcript would log 'ఈారు' as a meal. Piece density separates the cases by an order of magnitude:
0.0-0.7 pieces/s for non-speech against 7-10/s for speech (5.3/s for the English model on English).
`DefaultAsrEngine` refuses anything under 2.0 pieces/s as INPUT_NOT_USABLE. That threshold is a
knob, from synthetic data, and it is named as such in the code.

Density does not see a WRONG transcript: the 0 dB SNR row is at full density with two words wrong.
That is what the food matcher's NO_MATCH is for.

### Speed, and why it is not a number

Un-starved, the te model decoded a 3-4 s clip in 100-180 ms and the en model in 60-100 ms. The same
te clips took 640-1,100 ms in a later run and 2-6 s in another, during which this laptop had
452 MB of RAM free out of 16 GB and a 5x1024^2 numpy matmul took 400-800 ms instead of ~40. Those
slow rows are the host paging, not the model. The honest desktop statement is: the en model is
about ten times faster than the te and hi models on this CPU, and the te model is somewhere
between 0.03 and 0.5 of real time depending on what else the machine is doing. **None of it is a
phone number.** Rao's probe (`AsrDeviceTest`) produces the first one.

## What was decided

1. **sherpa-onnx is the ASR runtime, via the official release AAR, static-link variant.**
   sherpa-onnx is not on Maven Central (only a third-party repackaging is). The artefact is
   `sherpa-onnx-static-link-onnxruntime-1.13.8.aar`, 38,691,998 bytes, sha256
   `b22c3fc1b6a45666d28892bb2f7694beeb77a8362d7ebd77c1a5431ec9435471`, from
   `https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/releases/download/v1.13.8/`. Its arm64-v8a payload is one
   `libsherpa-onnx-jni.so` (24,169,352 B) with ONNX Runtime linked in: 133 exported symbols, all
   `Java_*`, no `Ort*`, `NEEDED` only libc/libm/libdl/liblog/libandroid. So it cannot collide with
   the probe's `onnxruntime-android` at packaging or at symbol resolution, which is the collision
   `0016` anticipated from the other variant. It lives at `app/libs/`, gitignored, and the demo
   APK's permission allowlist is unchanged with it in (exactly the three, `logs/asr-assemble.log`).
   Demo debug APK with it: 66,179,298 bytes, `libonnxruntime.so` absent.

2. **Language is a parameter, whole-utterance, energy VAD.** As the contract says. Endpointing is
   an energy detector with an adaptive floor rather than Silero VAD, because Silero is another
   model file through the arbiter and another row in `0013`'s table for a demo that is one person
   speaking one sentence; the ceiling (false endpoints in a loud room) and the upgrade are named in
   `EnergyEndpointer`. Every threshold is a knob and the recorded set tunes them.

3. **The confidence band is derived from the output, and says so.** sherpa-onnx's Kotlin result
   carries no acoustic score. HIGH means dense, in-vocabulary output; LOW means an `<unk>` piece;
   under 2 pieces/s is not speech. `LOW_ASR_CONFIDENCE` caps a figure at ROUGH, so LOW is not
   handed out on a hunch, and HIGH is documented as not meaning "the model is sure".

4. **The lease is taken after the recording, not across it.** A lease may not span waiting for a
   person (`ModelArbiter`), and a recording is that wait. `prepare()` preloads; the orchestrator's
   round-trip lease covers the rest.

5. **Staging layout**: `models/asr/<lang>/model.int8.onnx` with `tokens.txt` beside it, mirroring
   `data-sources/`. te and hi each get a copy of the shared root `tokens.txt`.

## What is measured, what is written, what is not

- MEASURED, desktop: everything above. 24 JVM tests, 0 failures, read from the JUnit XML
  (`logs/asr-unit-test.log`): endpointer state machine, event sequence, clip cut, density refusal,
  `<unk>` -> LOW, permission and busy failures, arbiter propagation of MODEL_LOAD_FAILED.
- WRITTEN, NEVER RUN: `AndroidAudioSource` (the microphone), `SherpaOnnxAsrLoader` on a device,
  `AsrDeviceTest`. The first two are thin by design; the third is the measurement.
- NOT MEASURED ANYWHERE: word error rate on anyone's actual voice; extraction accuracy from an ASR
  transcript; load time, decode time and resident cost on the phone; whether four threads is right
  on a 2+6 big.LITTLE part; whether ASR + LLM + TTS still fit with sherpa-onnx's feature extractor
  and decoder state resident (`0013`'s open floor).
